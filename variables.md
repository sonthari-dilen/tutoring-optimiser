| Variable | Category | Type | Scale | Justification |
|----------|----------|------|-------|---------------|
| session_type | Time | Categorical | 0–2 <br> 0: In-person <br> 1: Mixed <br> 2: Online | In-person sessions incur travel time and transport costs, reducing effective hourly rate. Online sessions allow back-to-back blocks, minimising unpaid transition time and maximising earnings per hour. |
| session_duration | Time | Ordinal | 1–4 <br> 1: 0.75 hr <br> 2: 1 hr <br> 3: 1.5 hr <br> 4: 2 hr | Longer sessions reduce the number of session transitions per week, lowering admin overhead per dollar earned. |
| session_perweek | Time | Ordinal | 1–4 <br> 1: Fortnightly <br> 2: 1 session/week <br> 3: 2 sessions/week <br> 4: 3+ sessions/week | More frequent sessions increase weekly income from a single client and reduce roster fragmentation. |
| session_cancellation_notice | Time | Ordinal | 1–5 <br> 1: No show <br> 2: Last-minute (<3 hr notice) <br> 3: Very late (<12 hr notice) <br> 4: Late (<24 hr notice) <br> 5: Early (≥24 hr notice) | Late cancellations create opportunity cost — the slot cannot be filled. No-shows are the most disruptive and signal low commitment. |
| session_cancellation_freq | Time | Ordinal | 1–3 <br> 1: Frequent (≥1/month) <br> 2: Occasional (<1/month, ≥1/quarter) <br> 3: Rare (<1/quarter) | Frequent cancellations produce irregular income and disrupt student progress, increasing prep rework. |
| rate_effective | Money | Quantitative | 1–5 <br> 1: <$35/hr <br> 2: $35–49/hr <br> 3: $50–64/hr <br> 4: $65–79/hr <br> 5: ≥$80/hr | Primary financial metric. Raw rate adjusted for travel time and prep time. Low effective rates are the highest burnout risk when combined with high admin or energy cost. |
| payment_timeliness | Money | Ordinal | 1–3 <br> 1: Frequently late or chased <br> 2: Occasionally late <br> 3: Always on time | Chasing payments is unpaid labour and mentally draining. Consistent late payment signals low respect for the working relationship. |
| agency_private | Money | Categorical | 0–1 <br> 0: Agency <br> 1: Private | Agency clients reduce take-home rate via commission and add administrative overhead. Private clients allow direct rate negotiation and higher margins. |
| admin_load | Time | Ordinal | 1–3 <br> 1: High (reports + invoicing + frequent emails) <br> 2: Medium (occasional reporting or invoicing) <br> 3: Low (session only, minimal admin) | Admin time is unpaid. High admin clients cost more than their hourly rate suggests. |
| parent_involvement | Energy | Ordinal | 1–5 <br> 1: Disengaged (no follow-through at home) <br> 2: Under-involved <br> 3: Appropriately involved <br> 4: Over-involved (frequent check-ins) <br> 5: Intrusive (undermines sessions) | Both extremes are problematic. Disengaged parents reduce student progress; overly involved parents increase communication load and can undermine the tutoring relationship. Optimal score is 3. Note: this variable should be reverse-scored in the model — 3 is ideal, not 5. |
| student_motivation | Energy | Ordinal | 1–5 <br> 1: Resistant (forced attendance) <br> 2: Passive <br> 3: Neutral <br> 4: Engaged <br> 5: Highly motivated | Student motivation is the strongest predictor of visible progress, which directly affects tutor satisfaction and sense of professional efficacy. |
| student_progress | Energy | Ordinal | 1–5 <br> 1: No visible progress <br> 2: Minimal progress <br> 3: Moderate progress <br> 4: Clear progress <br> 5: Exceptional progress | Progress reinforces purpose. Students who improve quickly are more rewarding to teach and generate positive word-of-mouth referrals. |
| rapport | Energy | Ordinal | 1–5 <br> 1: Poor (dreading sessions) <br> 2: Below average <br> 3: Neutral <br> 4: Good <br> 5: Excellent (genuinely enjoyable) | Subjective but consequential. Poor rapport compounds over time into burnout regardless of rate. Strong rapport makes even difficult sessions sustainable. |
| subject_complexity | Energy | Ordinal | 1–3 <br> 1: Requires significant relearning each session <br> 2: Occasional preparation needed <br> 3: Within comfortable expertise | Teaching outside your knowledge base adds invisible prep time and cognitive load. Subjects within your expertise zone are more efficient and more confident to deliver. |
| year_level | Money + Energy | Ordinal | 1–4 <br> 1: Primary (Yr 1–6) <br> 2: Lower secondary (Yr 7–9) <br> 3: Upper secondary (Yr 10–12) <br> 4: Tertiary | Higher year levels typically command higher rates and involve subjects closer to your own academic background, improving both income and session quality. |
| longevity_potential | Money | Ordinal | 1–3 <br> 1: Short-term (<6 months remaining, e.g. graduating Year 12) <br> 2: Medium-term (6–18 months) <br> 3: Long-term (>18 months, e.g. Year 7–10 student) | Long-term clients reduce acquisition effort and provide income stability. A Year 7 student represents potentially 5 years of consistent income. |
| referral_source | Marketing | Categorical | 0–2 <br> 0: Cold (agency or unknown) <br> 1: Platform (Cluey, TutorFinder) <br> 2: Word of mouth (existing client referral) | Referral source predicts client quality and fit. Word-of-mouth clients arrive pre-screened by someone who already knows your teaching style, resulting in better rapport and lower churn. Tracking this informs where to focus marketing. |
